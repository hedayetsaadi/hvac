Repair or replace? Five questions to ask first

Compare age, reliability, comfort, efficiency, and total cost before making a major decision.

In this articleSystem ageRepair historyComfort and efficiencyCompare proposals

No single rule determines whether repair or replacement is best. A reliable older system with an isolated problem may still be worth repairing, while repeated failures can change the calculation.

1. How old is the system?

Age provides context, not an automatic answer. Consider condition, maintenance history, available parts, and how the equipment has performed.

2. Are repairs becoming frequent?

Several repairs in a short period can indicate that other aging components may also need attention.

3. Is the home comfortable?

Uneven temperatures, humidity concerns, noise, and long run times may point to equipment, ductwork, controls, or home-envelope issues.

4. How does energy use compare?

Newer systems can improve efficiency, but savings depend on equipment selection, installation quality, local weather, and household habits.

5. What does each option include?

Compare repair cost with written replacement proposals that explain sizing, efficiency, ductwork changes, electrical work, warranties, and commissioning.
